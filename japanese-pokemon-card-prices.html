Japanese Pokémon card prices in TCG Portfolio

Japanese sets, each card priced in its own right.

Updated 3 November 2026

TCG Portfolio is a free Pokémon card collection tracker and price checker for iOS and Android that covers Japanese sets as well as English ones. Each Japanese card is priced in its own right, not converted from an English equivalent, with estimated ungraded and PSA graded values for the copies you own.

Does TCG Portfolio cover Japanese Pokémon sets?

Yes. TCG Portfolio carries card data in two languages: English and Japanese. Japanese sets get the same variant and condition detail as English sets, and each Japanese card is priced in its own right rather than converted from an English equivalent.

The app itself is in English. Japanese support means Japanese sets and cards, not a Japanese-language interface.

How do you find a Japanese set in the Explore tab?

Open the Explore tab and select the Japanese tab. TCG Portfolio then lists Japanese sets in a grid, grouped by series, with each set’s release month. Use the set search box to jump to a set by name, or scroll the grid.

Tap a set to see its cards, then open a card to reach its price page. If you already know the card, you can search for it by name instead of browsing by set.

Conditions & grades

What ungraded and PSA values can you check for Japanese cards?

TCG Portfolio prices Japanese cards in two condition categories: Ungraded and PSA.

  • Ungraded: Near Mint, Lightly Played, Moderately Played, Heavily Played and Damaged. Near Mint is the default.
  • PSA: grades 1 (PR) to 10 (GEM-MT). PSA 10 is the default.

Compare what a PSA 10 fetches against a PSA 9 or 8, and against an ungraded copy, before you buy a slab or submit an ungraded card. Each card page has an Ungraded/PSA toggle and lists prices by condition. Japanese cards use the same layout. The price chart can show Ungraded or any single PSA grade over 7D, 1M, 3M, 6M, YTD or 1Y.

Each printing is tracked separately by print run and finish. See how TCG Portfolio pricing works for the full list.

Why does the eBay search include the card’s language?

A Japanese card and its English counterpart are different items, and they often sell for different amounts. When you open eBay from a card page, TCG Portfolio adds the card’s language to the search, so a Japanese card opens a search for Japanese copies rather than English ones.

The card page links to both eBay sold listings and current eBay listings. Sold listings on the card page are free for everyone, so you can check the estimate against recent sales yourself.

What is the same and what differs from English cards?

The same:

  • Ungraded conditions from Near Mint to Damaged, and PSA grades 1 to 10.
  • Variant detail: each printing is labelled with its print run and finish.
  • The card page layout, with Price, Sold and Buy tabs and the same chart periods.
  • How you add a card: as an ungraded copy with its condition, or as a PSA graded slab.

What differs:

  • Japanese sets sit under their own Japanese tab in Explore.
  • Japanese cards are priced in their own right, not converted from an English equivalent.
  • The eBay search includes the card’s language, so it looks for Japanese copies.

Can you track English and Japanese cards in one collection?

Yes. TCG Portfolio keeps English and Japanese cards in one portfolio. Add each card as an ungraded copy with its condition, or as a PSA graded slab, with its print run and finish. Signed-in users can add cards a whole set page at a time.

The portfolio shows your total estimated collection value. Sort by most valuable, biggest gain or recently added, and filter by condition, variant and print run. Values show in USD by default, and you can choose another currency in Settings.

You can search and check prices without an account. Tracking a portfolio needs one, and your collection follows you to any device.

Can you add a whole Japanese set page at once?

Yes, if you are signed in. On a Japanese set screen in TCG Portfolio, long-press a card to start a selection, then choose the other cards on that page you own and add them in one go instead of opening each card in turn.

This helps when you are logging a sealed product you opened or working through a set you are completing. The cards join the same portfolio as your English cards, so the total value covers both languages.

Without an account you can still browse Japanese sets, search for cards and check their prices; only saving cards to a portfolio needs one.

Where do the Japanese card prices come from?

Prices are estimates compiled from third-party sources, including eBay and TCGplayer, and are not appraisals, offers or guarantees. Any figure can be inaccurate, delayed, incomplete or unavailable.

TCG Portfolio updates prices regularly, and new sets and pricing data are added frequently, so recently released Japanese sets appear in Explore over time.
